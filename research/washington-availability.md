## 1. Kalshi in Washington

**The core order.** Washington Attorney General Nick Brown sued KalshiEX, LLC on March 27, 2026 in King County Superior Court (Case No. 26-2-10264-3 SEA), alleging Kalshi's event contracts are illegal gambling under Washington's Gambling Act (RCW 9.46), Consumer Protection Act (RCW 19.86), and Recovery of Money Lost at Gambling Act (RCW 4.24.070). I read the actual **Order Granting Plaintiff State of Washington's Amended Motion for Preliminary Injunction**, signed by Judge John F. McHale on 07/17/2026 and filed 07/20/2026 ([primary source PDF](https://agportal-s3bucket.s3.us-west-2.amazonaws.com/Consumer%20Protection/Order%2020260720.pdf)).

- The order's Conclusions of Law define "Illegal Gambling Activities" narrowly as **"betting on sports, elections, politics, entertainment, culture, and mentions markets."** Kalshi's own platform categories, as listed in the court's Findings of Fact, are: Elections, Politics, Sports, Culture, Crypto, Commodities, Climate, Economics, Mentions, Finance, and Tech & Science. Only the first group is targeted.
- The court held the CEA does **not** preempt WA gambling law (no express preemption; field preemption only covers DCM *regulation*, not gambling law; no conflict preemption because Kalshi can comply with both regimes by geofencing WA).
- The July 17/20 order left implementation details ("specific terms of the Preliminary Injunction") to a follow-up order due **by August 5, 2026**. Secondary reporting (Washington State Standard, GeekWire, KUOW, Spokesman-Review, TechTimes — all dated Aug 13–14, 2026) describes that follow-on order as: banning sports, elections, politics, entertainment, culture, tech & science, and "mentions," while **explicitly preserving commodities, climate, economics, and finance contracts for Washington residents.** I could not independently re-fetch the August 13 amended order PDF directly (403 on the Washington State Standard page), so this later, more detailed scope is sourced to consistent secondary reporting rather than a primary document I personally read — flagging as **secondary-sourced, high-confidence given cross-corroboration** (Washington State Standard, KUOW, GeekWire, TechTimes, komonews, Spokesman-Review all agree independently).
- **Geofencing deadlines** (secondary-sourced, consistent across outlets): IP-address/residency-based geofence by **August 19, 2026**; multi-source (GeoComply-style) geofencing by **September 2, 2026**; one outlet (TechTimes) cites a **$120,000/day** penalty for missing the Sept. 2 deadline — UNVERIFIED against a primary document.
- **Weather/climate — explicit answer:** Every secondary source I checked (Washington State Standard, KUOW, GeekWire, TechTimes, komonews) states climate/commodities/economics/finance markets **remain available** to Washington residents. Weather (daily high temperature) markets are Kalshi's "Climate" category, so they are **not** covered by the injunction as reported. I did not find a source that names "weather" or "temperature" markets by that specific term — the carve-out is stated at the "Climate" category level, which is where Kalshi houses its weather/temperature contracts per its own [Weather Markets help article](https://help.kalshi.com/en/articles/13823837-weather-markets) (that article itself says nothing about state restrictions).
- **Kalshi's own help center**: I checked [help.kalshi.com's "How is Kalshi regulated?"](https://help.kalshi.com/en/articles/13823765-how-is-kalshi-regulated) and the [Weather Markets](https://help.kalshi.com/en/articles/13823837-weather-markets) article directly — **neither mentions Washington, state-by-state restrictions, or geofencing.** I could not locate a Kalshi help-center article that lists restricted states; Kalshi's regulatory page (kalshi.com/regulatory) is referenced by third parties but I did not find help-center language addressing this specific dispute. **This is a gap** — Kalshi does not appear to publish a consumer-facing "restricted states" article; users learn of blocks via in-app notices (reported, not independently verified by me) rather than published policy.
- **Appeal/stay status**: Kalshi sought an emergency stay from the Washington Court of Appeals; a commissioner denied it, and the trial judge separately denied reconsideration. Separately, the Ninth Circuit denied Kalshi's stay request in a related federal preemption challenge around May 21, 2026, finding Kalshi had not shown a strong likelihood of success or irreparable harm ([DeFi Rate](https://defirate.com/news/ninth-circuit-denies-kalshi-stay-washington-possible-supreme-court-appeal/)). Kalshi is reported to be considering a Supreme Court petition; no filing date found (**UNVERIFIED**). As of Sept. 2, 2026, Washington and NADEX/OG.com entered a stipulation pausing enforcement against OG.com pending the Ninth Circuit's resolution of the preemption question — Kalshi has argued (per Covers.com, "selective non-enforcement" story, Sept 2, 2026) it should get the same treatment; as of this writing I found no confirmation WA extended the same pause to Kalshi.
- **Could this expand to all event contracts?** The order's own preemption reasoning (states retain gambling-law authority over DCM-listed contracts case-by-case; CEA preempts only DCM *regulation*, not gambling-law enforcement) is written broadly enough that, in principle, Washington could extend it to any category it later characterizes as "gambling" — the court explicitly reasons that "certain types of contracts... may be allowed in some states and not in others" contract-by-contract. However, the currently entered order **only** covers the enumerated categories; nothing I found shows a pending motion to expand it to climate/economics/finance. This is my **inference**, not a stated fact in any source — flagging as such.

## 2. Polymarket and other CFTC-regulated venues

**Polymarket (international/crypto-wallet site).** Per its own [Help Center article on Geographic Restrictions](https://help.polymarket.com/en/articles/13364163-geographic-restrictions), the US is one of **39 countries "completely restricted"** from the platform — no new orders, and unlike a "close-only" tier applied to some other jurisdictions (e.g., Singapore, Thailand), the US does not get even close-only access; it is a full block. Polymarket's Terms of Service separately prohibit US persons from trading and prohibit circumventing geoblocks (Section 2.1.4 per secondary summaries — I did not independently pull the full ToS text). This is a platform-wide rule; it is not Washington-specific, and I found no evidence WA has taken any enforcement action against Polymarket's international site (it isn't a named defendant in the Kalshi litigation).

**Polymarket US (QCX LLC, d/b/a Polymarket US).** The CFTC issued an **Amended Order of Designation on November 25, 2025** ([CFTC filing](https://www.cftc.gov/media/12806/Polymarket%20US%20Amended%20Order%20of%20Designation/download)) permitting QCX to operate as a Designated Contract Market with intermediated access via FCMs, with new rules effective December 10, 2025. Secondary sources describe it as available "in over 40 states" and as a fully separate entity/platform from the international site. I found **no primary WA source** (no AG press release, no help-center notice) addressing Polymarket US specifically. Given the King County court's reasoning applies to any DCM offering the enumerated "gambling-like" categories, Polymarket US could face the same theory of liability if it lists sports/politics-style contracts to WA residents — but **this is my inference**; no such action has been filed against it as far as I could find. **UNVERIFIED**: exact category list Polymarket US currently offers, and whether it offers weather/temperature markets at all.

**Other CFTC-regulated venues (brief, mostly UNVERIFIED for WA specifics):**
- **OG.com** (North American Derivatives Exchange, "NADEX," rebranded/operating as OG) — subject to an Aug 18, 2026 non-enforcement stipulation with WA regulators pending Ninth Circuit resolution of the preemption question. This is a **temporary prosecutorial pause, not a legal ruling that OG.com is compliant.**
- **Robinhood event contracts** — Robinhood does not operate its own DCM; it routes orders to Kalshi, ForecastEx, OG.com, and "Rothera" (a joint venture involving Susquehanna). Its WA availability for any given contract inherits whichever backend venue is used and that venue's WA restrictions; I found no Robinhood-specific WA statement.

## 3. Washington gambling law background (RCW 9.46) and how it applied

- Washington has regulated gambling since 1889 and, per RCW 9.46.240 (2006 amendment), effectively **banned all online gambling**, with a narrow 2020 carve-out for tribal-land sports wagering under tribal-state compacts.
- RCW 9.46.0237 defines gambling as "staking or risking something of value upon the outcome of a contest of chance or a future contingent event not under the person's control" — the AG's theory is that Kalshi's binary yes/no contracts (bought between $0.01–$0.99, paying $1 to the winner) fit this definition regardless of federal characterization as a "swap" or "event contract."
- On **December 9, 2025**, the Washington State Gambling Commission issued a public notice stating: **"Offering events-based contracts or participating in these markets is not authorized in Washington State"** ([wsgc.wa.gov](https://wsgc.wa.gov/news/2025/prediction-markets)). The court's order treats Kalshi's continued operation after this notice as evidence of "willful" disregard, supporting the injunction.
- The court's preemption analysis (Section II of the order) is the crux of the case: it holds the CEA's grant of "exclusive jurisdiction" to the CFTC over DCM transactions does not preempt state gambling law, relying on statutory savings clauses for state "regulatory authorities" and state courts, and on the CEA's own "Special Rule" (7 U.S.C. § 7a-2(c)(5)(C)(i)(I)) referencing contracts involving activity "unlawful under any... State law" — reasoned by the court to mean Congress contemplated that some event contracts could be lawful in some states and not others.
- This is a **trial-court preliminary injunction**, not a final appellate ruling; Kalshi disputes the preemption analysis and is pursuing further appeal (see Section 1). Nevada has reached a similar result via its Gaming Control Board and courts (extended preliminary injunction against Kalshi sports/election contracts, first entered ~April 2026); Michigan has also entered a temporary shutdown order. No source suggests any state court has yet ruled *against* a state's authority to restrict event contracts on preemption grounds (the closest countervailing signal is the pending Ninth Circuit appeal and Kalshi's still-unresolved Supreme Court option).

## 4. Does paper trading / reading public market data raise legal issues?

**Expected answer confirmed, with a nuance.** I found no source — statute, AG filing, court order, or platform ToS — suggesting that merely *viewing* public market data (prices, order books) or maintaining a personal paper-trading log/simulation is itself gambling or otherwise unlawful in Washington. The King County order and RCW 9.46 both target the act of **staking/risking money** on a contingent outcome; paper trading involves no money at risk and no counterparty payout, so it falls outside the statutory definition of gambling (RCW 9.46.0237) on its face.

**Nuance:** 
- If reading data requires creating or logging into an account on a platform whose ToS bars WA residents from that category (e.g., Polymarket's international site bars all US persons, not just for real-money trading), then merely holding/using an account could violate that platform's *Terms of Service* — a contractual matter, not a gambling-law violation, but still worth avoiding. Kalshi's data for allowed categories (weather, econ) should not present this issue since those categories are open to WA accounts per the current order.
- This note does not constitute legal advice; it reflects the absence of any source suggesting otherwise.
